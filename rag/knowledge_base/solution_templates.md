## Solving a Quadratic Equation
**Step 1:** Identify $a$, $b$, and $c$ in the standard form $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$.
**Step 2:** Start with the quadratic formula: $x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}$
**Step 3:** Calculate the discriminant, $D = b^2 - 4ac$.
**Step 4:** Substitute $D$ into the formula.
**Step 5:** Find the two roots.

## Computing Conditional Probability (Bayes)
**Step 1:** Define the events clearly. Let $A =$ event of interest, $B =$ condition given.
**Step 2:** Write down the known probabilities, $P(A)$, $P(B|A)$, and prior probabilities of complementary events.
**Step 3:** Apply Bayes' Theorem: $P(A|B) = \frac{P(B|A)P(A)}{P(B|A)P(A) + P(B|A')P(A')}$
**Step 4:** Substitute and calculate. Let the denominator represent the total probability of event $B$.

## Finding Maxima/Minima Using Derivatives
**Step 1:** Find the first derivative of the function, $f'(x)$.
**Step 2:** Set the first derivative equal to 0 and solve for $x$ to find critical points.
**Step 3:** Check for points where the derivative undefined.
**Step 4:** Use the second derivative test. Find $f''(x)$ and evaluate it at each critical point $c$:
- If $f''(c) < 0$, it is a local maximum.
- If $f''(c) > 0$, it is a local minimum.
- If $f''(c) = 0$, the test is inconclusive.

## Solving a 2x2 System of Equations
Using Elimination:
**Step 1:** Write the equations aligned by variables.
**Step 2:** Multiply one or both equations by a constant so that the coefficients of one variable are opposites.
**Step 3:** Add the modified equations to eliminate one variable.
**Step 4:** Solve the resulting equation for the remaining variable.
**Step 5:** Substitute that value back into one of original equations to find the second variable.

## Computing a Definite Integral by Substitution
**Step 1:** Choose an expression for $u$. Let $u = g(x)$.
**Step 2:** Find $du$. Compute the derivative $g'(x)$ and write $du = g'(x)dx$.
**Step 3:** Substitute into the integral replacing $x$ terms with $u$ terms. Check that $u$ definitions apply correctly.
**Step 4:** Change the limits of integration. When $x = a, u = g(a)$ and when $x = b, u = g(b)$. Apply those to substitution limits.
**Step 5:** Integrate the new expression with respect to $u$.
**Step 6:** Substitute the limits directly into the result to find the definite integral value.
